The Warm Bread

📖 Reading

It was a difficult week for Sama. Her grades were slipping, and she felt overwhelmed by all her schoolwork. On Thursday afternoon, she sat by her window, staring at the grey sky that seemed to mirror her mood. She hadn't spoken to many people lately and felt isolated from her friends.

Then, there was a knock at the door. It was Mrs. Aya, the friendly neighbor who lived upstairs. She held a small basket covered with a clean white cloth, still warm from the oven.

"I baked some fresh bread this morning," Mrs. Aya said warmly. "I thought you might like some."

The smell was irresistible, wrapping around Sama like a gentle hug. Sama smiled for the first time in days and invited her in for tea. They sat together, chatting about small things - school, plants, and Mrs. Aya's garden - while the soft sound of rain tapped on the window. Slowly, Sama felt her worries began to fade.

Before leaving, Mrs. Aya said, "Sometimes, the smallest kindness can make a big difference."

Sama kept thinking about those words. She decided to help her younger cousin with homework that evening. Seeing her cousin's face light up filled her with optimism.

She realized that kindness doesn't just make others feel better; it can change your own heart, too.

Planting Trees, Growing Myself

📖 Reading

Ahmed joined a tree-planting project in his village. At first, he just wanted to help his community. But as days passed, he discovered that volunteering transformed him in ways he never expected.

The team worked hard, planting saplings along the roads and in the school yard. An old farmer told Ahmed, "A tree is like a friend. You take care of it, and it gives you shade and fresh air."

Ahmed felt his confidence grow with every tree he planted. He learned that volunteering helps the volunteer as much as it helps others.

Serving the Community in Times of Danger

📖 Reading

My name is Radwan, and I work as a firefighter in Alexandria. Many people think our job is just putting out fires, but it's much more than that. We rescue people from car accidents, help during floods, and sometimes save animals trapped in dangerous places.

The truth is, our work can be risky. I have entered burning buildings where smoke made it hard to see and breathe. I've stood on shaking ladders in the middle of the night, with the heat almost burning my skin. Sadly, I have also seen lives lost before we could reach them. These moments stay with me forever.

Yet, we do it because we believe in obligation - the duty to help each other, no matter the cost. In every emergency, we must act quickly, stay calm, and trust our training. We don't think about fear in those moments; we think about the people waiting for us.

I am proud to serve my community. I know that my work saves lives, and that is worth every risk. When you help others, you are not just doing a job - you are protecting hope itself.

Show, Don't Just Tell (Descriptive Essay)

✍️ Descriptive Essay Structure

Introduction

Introduce the person, place, or thing. Give a general idea of why it's important to you.

"My grandpa's house is a place where time stands still."

Body Paragraphs

Each paragraph focuses on a different sense or aspect (sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch).

"The smell of freshly baked bread fills the kitchen every morning."

Conclusion

Summarize the main ideas and share your overall feeling about the topic.

"My grandpa's house will always be my favorite place on earth."

✍️ Writing Task

اكتب مقال وصفي من 120-150 كلمة عن "My Grandpa's House".

Grammar: Modal Verbs

Should / Shouldn't

Use should/shouldn't for suggestions and advice.

You should always take a shower every day in hot weather.
You shouldn't take your umbrella today. It's not sunny.

Must / Mustn't

Use must/mustn't for rules, laws, strong advice, and warm invitations.

Drivers must follow traffic rules.
Nouran mustn't waste her time. Exams are nearby!
You must come and try mom's cake. It is great!

Have to

Use have to when you mean you have no choice (external obligation).

I have to renew my driving licence next month.
We have to stay late at work. The manager ordered us.

Key Notes

❌ must to (wrong)

✅ must + base verb (correct)

❌ should to call (wrong)

✅ should call (correct)
